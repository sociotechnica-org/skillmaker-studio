## WHAT

The buyer/persona map from the 2026-08-03 Danvers catch-up, set against a
**full-lifecycle** framing of the product: research → skill writing →
evals → publish → distribution → analytics → permissioning → cost
tracking → audits. Today's product covers the first four legs; the map
records who is suspected to pay for which leg.

- **Ops / legal / consistency-minded**: want the product to WRITE skills,
  participating in research ("at our company we do it this way");
  predictability is the value.
- **Technical buyers** (prompts in production, neck on the line): evals
  and the lifecycle — the early-adopter profile. "Help them solve an
  emergency, then help them build a system so the emergencies stop
  happening."
- **Non-technical / operational buyers**: distribution ("stupid simple
  one-click ways to get skills on their laptop"), analytics (installs,
  runs, power users), cost savings — and may not care about evals at
  all: "Didn't the AI write the skill? … Don't overwhelm me with this
  eval stuff."

## WHY

Two honest tensions the map puts on record rather than resolves. First,
the director's own doubt: "we're definitely leaning heavily on skill
writing and skill evaling right now, but I'm not even sure those are the
most valuable parts of the product that people are gonna care about."
Second, the teaching burden: some buyers "care very much about the front
end of the product [and] maybe didn't even know that an eval was a
thing" — evals is the most technical part of the product, and the
persona least served by it may be the one distribution features would
win. Neither observation changes a ruling today (D8 still demands felt
need from real use before building the later legs) — but future
prioritization arguments should cite this map instead of re-deriving it.

## HOW

Positioning shorthand from the same call, useful when writing surfaces:
"selling insurance, not rocket ships" — model releases and provider
volatility are the weather; the surfaces that SHOW stability or
regression (evals, audits, scorecards) are the insurance artifacts. The
later lifecycle legs align with the hosted ladder recorded on
[[../machine/Concept - Hosted-ASAP Intent]].
